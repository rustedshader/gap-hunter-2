## 3. Data Handling and Classification *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

3. Data Handling and Classification

3.1 Data Classification
All company data shall be classified according to the established data classification scheme, which includes, but is not limited to, Public and Confidential.

3.2 Classification Definitions
3.2.1 Public: Information freely available to anyone (e.g., marketing materials).
3.2.2 Confidential: Information requiring protection due to sensitivity, including but not limited to customer data, employee records, and financial data.

3.3 Data Handling Requirements
3.3.1 Protection of Confidential Data: All systems, processes, and personnel handling Confidential data must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of that data.
3.3.2 Access Control: Access to Confidential data shall be granted strictly on a need-to-know basis, based on documented authorization.
3.3.3 Data Lifecycle Management: The organization shall establish and maintain procedures for the entire data lifecycle, including creation, storage, use, retention, and secure disposal of all data.

3.4 Policy Review
This policy and the data classification scheme shall be reviewed and updated whenever the organizational mission statement is formally changed or when significant changes in legal or regulatory requirements necessitate a revision.

## 4. Stakeholder Cybersecurity Risk Management *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

4. Stakeholder Cybersecurity Risk Management

4.1 Objective
To ensure that the organization understands, considers, and addresses the cybersecurity risk management needs and expectations of all relevant internal and external stakeholders.

4.2 Stakeholder Identification and Analysis
The Information Security Officer (or designated role) shall establish and maintain a register of all relevant internal and external stakeholders with respect to cybersecurity risk management.

4.3 Expectation Documentation
The organization shall actively identify, document, and formally record the specific cybersecurity expectations, requirements, and concerns of each identified stakeholder group.

4.4 Expectation Tracking and Review
The organization shall establish a process to continuously monitor and track changes in stakeholder cybersecurity expectations. This process shall include, but not be limited to:

1. Reviewing stakeholder expectations during the annual policy review cycle.
2. Establishing formal communication channels for stakeholders to submit feedback regarding risk management practices.
3. Reassessing and updating the documented expectations whenever significant organizational, regulatory, or technological changes occur.

4.5 Policy Alignment
All Information Security policies, standards, and procedures shall be reviewed and updated to ensure they explicitly address and satisfy the documented cybersecurity expectations of the identified stakeholders.

## 6. Critical Business Service Documentation and Prioritization *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

6. Critical Business Service Documentation and Prioritization

6.1 Objective

To ensure that the organization formally identifies, documents, and prioritizes the recovery of critical business services in the event of a security incident, thereby ensuring the timely restoration of essential organizational functions.

6.2 Critical Business Service Identification

The Information Technology (IT) department, in coordination with Business Unit leaders, shall maintain a current and accurate inventory of all business services deemed critical to the organization's mission and operations.

6.3 Documentation Requirements

The IT department shall formally document the following for each identified critical business service:

6.3.1 Service Description: A clear, unambiguous description of the business function provided by the service.

6.3.2 Supporting Technology: Identification of all systems, applications, infrastructure components, and data repositories required to support the service.

6.3.3 Criticality Rating: Each critical business service shall be assigned a criticality rating (e.g., High, Medium, Low) based on the potential impact to the organization if the service is unavailable.

6.4 Incident Response Prioritization

The Incident Response Plan (IRP) shall incorporate a defined procedure for prioritizing the recovery and restoration of critical business services following a security incident.

6.4.1 Prioritization Mandate: In the event of a security incident, the Incident Response Team (IRT) shall prioritize the restoration of services based on the documented criticality ratings established in Section 6.3.

6.4.2 Recovery Procedures: Recovery procedures documented within the Incident Response Plan must explicitly detail the steps required to restore the most critical services first, ensuring that essential business functions are restored within defined recovery time objectives (RTOs).

6.5 Stakeholder Communication

The Security and IT leadership shall establish a process to regularly communicate the status of critical business services to relevant stakeholders.

6.5.1 Reporting Frequency: Critical service status reports shall be generated and distributed at least [Specify Frequency, e.g., weekly] to executive leadership and relevant operational managers.

6.5.2 Communication Content: Reports shall include the current operational status of critical services, any known impacts, and the projected timeline for recovery, as determined by the Incident Response Team.

## 7. Data Handling and Retention Requirements *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

7. Data Handling and Retention Requirements

7.1 Objective
To establish mandatory procedures for the secure handling, storage, access control, and retention of all company and customer data, ensuring compliance with regulatory requirements and the principles outlined in the Information Security Policy.

7.2 Data Classification and Handling
7.2.1 All data shall be classified according to the established Data Classification scheme (Public, Confidential) as defined in Section 2.0. 
7.2.2 Personnel shall handle data strictly according to its classification level. Access to Confidential data shall be restricted solely to personnel with a documented business need to know.

7.3 Data Storage and Protection
7.3.1 All Confidential data shall be stored in approved, encrypted systems. Encryption mechanisms shall be applied to data both at rest and in transit.
7.3.2 Data retention periods shall be defined based on legal, regulatory, and business requirements. Data shall not be retained longer than necessary for the specified purpose.

7.4 Data Disposal
7.4.1 When data reaches the end of its required retention period, the data owner shall initiate a documented and auditable process for secure data disposal, ensuring that all copies are permanently and irrecoverably destroyed in accordance with data sanitization standards.

7.5 Compliance and Monitoring
7.5.1 All data handling and disposal activities shall be logged and monitored to ensure compliance with this policy and applicable legal mandates. Audit logs shall be retained for a period defined by the Data Retention Schedule.

## 8. Risk Appetite and Tolerance *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

8. Risk Appetite and Tolerance

8.1 Objective
This section establishes the mandatory requirements for defining, documenting, communicating, and managing the organization's risk appetite and risk tolerance to ensure that security and risk management activities align with the organization's strategic objectives and risk management framework.

8.2 Risk Appetite Definition
The organization shall establish a formal, documented statement of its risk appetite, which defines the level and types of risk the organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives. This statement shall be approved by executive leadership.

8.3 Risk Tolerance Definition
The organization shall establish specific risk tolerance levels for defined risk categories. Risk tolerance shall define the acceptable boundaries for deviations from the established risk appetite.

8.4 Risk Categorization and Alignment
Risk appetite and tolerance statements shall be categorized based on relevant risk types, including but not limited to operational risk, financial risk, reputational risk, and compliance risk. Each category shall be explicitly linked to relevant business objectives and strategic goals.

8.5 Communication and Review
The documented risk appetite and tolerance statements shall be communicated to all relevant stakeholders, including executive leadership, risk owners, and relevant operational teams. These statements shall be formally reviewed and revalidated upon significant organizational changes, major strategic shifts, or at least annually.

8.6 Risk Management Integration
Risk appetite and tolerance statements shall serve as the foundational input for the organization's risk management processes, including risk identification, assessment, treatment, and ongoing monitoring. All risk management activities shall be measured against these defined tolerances.

8.7 Documentation
All risk appetite and tolerance statements, associated risk categories, and the documented process for managing them shall be maintained in a centralized, accessible repository.

## 9. Information Security Policy Requirements *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

9. Information Security Policy Requirements

9.1 Purpose
This section establishes the mandatory requirements for the development, maintenance, and enforcement of the Information Security Policy, ensuring that all data handling practices align with organizational security objectives and regulatory obligations.

9.2 Policy Scope
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, vendors, and third parties who access, process, or manage the organization's information assets.

9.3 Policy Development and Approval
1. The Information Security Policy shall be developed by the Information Security team in coordination with relevant business units.
2. The draft policy must be reviewed and approved by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) or designated authority prior to official issuance.

9.4 Policy Maintenance and Review
1. The Information Security Policy shall be reviewed and updated at least annually, or upon significant changes to organizational structure, technology, or regulatory requirements.
2. Any changes to the policy must be documented, approved through the established change management process, and communicated to all relevant stakeholders.

9.5 Policy Enforcement
1. All personnel must comply with the provisions of the Information Security Policy.
2. Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract, in accordance with organizational HR procedures.

9.6 Policy Ownership
The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) shall be the owner of the Information Security Policy and shall be responsible for its ongoing management and enforcement.

## 15. Supplier Identification, Prioritization, and Oversight *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

15. Supplier Identification, Prioritization, and Oversight

15.1 Objective
To establish a formal process for identifying, assessing, and managing risks associated with third-party suppliers and vendors to ensure the security and integrity of the organization's information systems and data.

15.2 Supplier Inventory and Identification
1. The organization shall maintain a comprehensive, up-to-date inventory of all third-party suppliers and vendors with whom the organization engages in business or data exchange.
2. For each identified supplier, the organization shall document the following information:
    a. Full legal name and contact information.
    b. Scope of services provided and data types accessed or processed.
    c. Contractual agreements defining security responsibilities and compliance obligations.

3. Suppliers categorized as High Criticality (based on the risk assessment) shall be subject to enhanced security monitoring, mandatory annual security assessments, and stricter contractual controls.
4. Suppliers categorized as Medium or Low Criticality shall be subject to standard security monitoring and periodic review as defined in Section [Reference to relevant monitoring policy].

15.4 Monitoring and Review
1. The organization shall review the security posture and compliance status of all suppliers on a periodic basis, at least annually, or upon significant changes to the scope of work or identified risk factors.
2. Monitoring activities shall include verification of the supplier's adherence to the security requirements stipulated in their contractual agreements.
3. Any identified deviations or risks in a supplier's security posture shall be formally documented, assessed for impact, and remediated according to the organization's risk management procedures.

## 16. Supply Chain Risk Management in Contracts *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

16. Supply Chain Risk Management in Contracts

16.1 Objective

To ensure that cybersecurity risks associated with third-party suppliers and vendors are identified, managed, and mitigated through legally binding contractual obligations.

16.2 Contractual Requirements

All contracts entered into with suppliers or third parties that involve the processing, storage, or transmission of Company or Customer data shall incorporate specific cybersecurity risk management clauses.

16.2.1 Security Standards

Contracts must define minimum acceptable cybersecurity standards that the supplier shall adhere to, which shall be commensurate with the criticality of the data being handled and the supplier's role in the supply chain.

16.2.2 Breach Notification

Contracts must establish clear, legally enforceable timelines and procedures for the supplier to notify the Customer of any suspected or actual security incidents or data breaches involving the data.

16.2.3 Audit and Review Rights

Contracts shall grant the Customer the right to conduct, or require the Customer to conduct, security assessments, audits, or reviews of the supplier’s security controls relevant to the scope of the contract.

16.2.4 Risk Prioritization

Contracts must require the supplier to identify, assess, and prioritize cybersecurity risks related to their operations and data handling, and to provide a documented plan for risk treatment and mitigation.

16.2.5 Review and Approval

All contracts incorporating these requirements shall be subject to review and approval by the Legal and Information Security departments prior to execution to ensure requirements are proportional to the supplier's criticality.

## 21. Supplier Relationship and Offboarding Management *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

21. Supplier Relationship and Offboarding Management

21.1 Objective
To establish a formal, systematic process for managing the risks associated with third-party suppliers and service providers throughout the lifecycle of a business relationship, ensuring that security and access controls are appropriately managed upon contract conclusion or termination.

21.2 Supplier Risk Management
All contracts with suppliers or service providers that involve access to, or processing of, Confidential or Public information shall include explicit provisions detailing security obligations, data handling requirements, and mandatory exit procedures.

21.3 Supplier Offboarding Procedures
The organization shall implement and maintain a documented Supplier Offboarding Procedure that addresses the following requirements for all departing suppliers or service agreements:

21.3.1 Data Handling and Destruction: The organization shall ensure that all data, including copies, backups, and derived information, provided to or created by the supplier is returned to the organization or securely destroyed in accordance with data retention and disposal policies prior to the termination of the agreement.

21.3.2 Access Revocation: Upon contract termination or expiration, the organization shall immediately initiate procedures to revoke all access rights, credentials, system access, and physical access granted to the supplier across all organizational systems, applications, and facilities.

21.3.3 Key and Credential Management: The organization shall mandate the immediate rotation or revocation of all cryptographic keys, access tokens, passwords, and authentication credentials associated with the supplier's access.

21.3.4 Knowledge Transfer and Documentation: The organization shall require the supplier to provide a documented knowledge transfer plan, ensuring that all necessary institutional knowledge, operational procedures, and system documentation related to the services provided are transferred to the organization or an approved successor.

21.3.5 Contractual Requirements: All new and existing supplier contracts shall explicitly incorporate mandatory clauses requiring adherence to the procedures outlined in this section, including defined timelines for data return, access revocation, and knowledge transfer.

21.4 Monitoring and Review
The Security and Compliance team shall periodically review the effectiveness of the Supplier Offboarding Procedures by auditing a sample of completed offboarding processes to ensure compliance with all contractual and internal security requirements.

## 24. Supplier Identification and Assessment *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

24. Supplier Identification and Assessment

24.1 Objective

To ensure that the organization only engages with and contracts with suppliers and vendors who meet the necessary security and compliance standards required to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of organizational information.

24.2 Critical Supplier Definition

24.2.1 Critical suppliers shall be defined as any external entity or vendor whose services, products, or systems provide access to, process, store, or manage the organization's Confidential data, or whose failure or compromise could result in a significant impact to the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the organization's information systems or services.

24.2.2 Criteria for Critical Supplier Designation

24.2.2.1 Critical suppliers shall be identified based on a risk assessment that considers the sensitivity of the data accessed, the criticality of the service provided, and the potential impact of a security incident.

24.2.2.2 The Information Security Officer (or designated delegate) shall maintain a documented register of all identified critical suppliers, including the rationale for their designation.

24.3 Supplier Assessment Requirements

24.3.1 Pre-Acquisition Assessment

Before entering into any contractual agreement or initiating a significant engagement with a critical supplier, the organization shall conduct a formal security assessment.

24.3.2 Required Evidence

The organization shall require critical suppliers to provide documented evidence demonstrating compliance with relevant security controls, which may include, but is not limited to:

(a) Current, valid certifications, such as SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001, relevant to the scope of the services provided.

(b) Completed, organization-specific security questionnaires addressing controls relevant to the data being processed or stored.

## 25. Data in Transit Protection *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

25. Data in Transit Protection

25.1 Objective

To ensure the confidentiality and integrity of all data transmitted across organizational and external networks.

25.2 Encryption Requirements

All data transmitted over public or untrusted networks shall be protected using cryptographic protocols that meet or exceed the current industry standards.

25.3 Protocol Enforcement

25.3.1 The organization shall enforce the use of Transport Layer Security (TLS) version 1.2 or higher for all external and internal communications involving sensitive data.

25.3.2 Legacy and insecure protocols, including but not limited to SSLv3, TLS 1.0, and TLS 1.1, shall be explicitly disabled and prohibited for all network services.

25.4 Certificate Management

25.4.1 The organization shall implement a formal process for the lifecycle management of all digital certificates used for data encryption.

25.4.2 Certificate expiration dates shall be monitored, and renewal procedures shall be established to prevent the use of expired or revoked certificates.

25.5 Secure Channel Usage

25.5.1 Sensitive data, including but not limited to Confidential data (as defined in POL-DATA-002), shall only be transmitted over established, encrypted channels, such as Virtual Private Networks (VPNs) or approved secure communication protocols.

25.5.2 Remote access to systems handling sensitive data shall mandate the use of encrypted VPNs.

25.6 Monitoring

25.6.1 Systems shall be implemented to monitor network traffic for evidence of unencrypted data transmission or the use of prohibited legacy protocols.

25.6.2 Monitoring activities shall be performed regularly to detect and respond to potential violations of this policy.

## 26. Data Inventory and Metadata Management *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

26. Data Inventory and Metadata Management

26.1 Objective

This section establishes the requirements for maintaining an accurate inventory of all company data and associated metadata to ensure proper management, protection, and lifecycle control, in alignment with the requirements of NIST CSF ID.AM-7.

26.2 Data Inventory Requirements

All systems and data repositories containing company information shall be inventoried to identify the location, type, and sensitivity of the data.

26.3 Metadata Requirements

For every identified data asset, the following metadata shall be documented and maintained in a centralized repository:

26.3.1 Data Owner: The designated individual or role accountable for the data.
26.3.2 Data Location: The physical or logical location where the data is stored.
26.3.3 Data Classification: The classification level assigned to the data (e.g., Public, Confidential, Restricted).
26.3.4 Retention Schedule: The defined schedule for data retention and disposition.
26.3.5 Access Controls: The established access controls and permissions governing access to the data.

26.4 Data Discovery and Review

26.4.1 Discovery Exercise: The organization shall conduct a formal data discovery exercise at least annually, or upon significant changes to data systems or regulatory requirements, to identify and validate the inventory of all data assets.

26.4.2 Validation: The results of the data discovery exercise shall be reviewed and validated by the Data Owner to ensure the accuracy of the documented metadata.

26.5 Integration with Security Tools

26.5.1 Tool Integration: Data inventory and metadata shall be integrated with existing Data Loss Prevention (DLP) and privacy management tools to facilitate automated enforcement of classification policies and retention schedules.

26.5.2 Enforcement: Automated processes shall be implemented to enforce the access controls and retention schedules documented in Section 26.3 across all data repositories.

## 27. Data-at-Rest Encryption and Key Management *(NEW — added per gap analysis)*

27. Data-at-Rest Encryption and Key Management

27.1 Objective

To ensure the confidentiality and integrity of all data, regardless of its storage location, by mandating encryption for all data at rest and establishing secure controls for the management of encryption keys.

27.2 Data Encryption Requirements

All data classified as Confidential or Public that is stored in any persistent storage medium, including but not limited to databases, file systems, operating system volumes, backup media, and cloud storage services, shall be protected by encryption.

27.3 Encryption Standards

Encryption utilized for data at rest shall employ industry-recognized, strong cryptographic algorithms, such as AES-256, and shall be implemented according to current cryptographic standards.

27.4 Key Management

27.4.1 Key Protection: Encryption keys shall be protected using robust cryptographic mechanisms, including access controls, separation of duties, and secure storage mechanisms appropriate to the sensitivity of the data they protect.

27.4.2 Key Lifecycle Management: Procedures shall be established to govern the entire lifecycle of encryption keys, including generation, distribution, storage, rotation, backup, and secure destruction.

27.4.3 Access Control: Access to encryption keys shall be strictly controlled based on the principle of least privilege. Access to decryption keys must be granted only to authorized personnel and systems requiring access to the underlying data.

27.5 Backup Integrity

All data backups containing encrypted data shall maintain the integrity of the encryption mechanism. Backup procedures shall include verification steps to ensure that the encrypted data remains accessible and uncorrupted upon restoration.
